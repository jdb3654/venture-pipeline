# Owner brief

Written October 10, 2026 from an interview with the owner. This is the source of truth for what we are trying to do. If anything else in the repo conflicts with it, this file wins until the owner changes it.

## The goal

Build a continuously refilled portfolio of small, legal online businesses (apps, websites, small SaaS, tools) that gradually replace the owner's employment income. Then keep some of them running at low maintenance into retirement.

- Current total compensation is about $208k a year. The owner expects the job may not last more than about two years, and plans to work another 8–10 years.
- Replacement is gradual. Nobody expects the first product to replace a salary.
- Staged milestones (each one is a real success):
  1. First paying customer.
  2. Covering the portfolio's own costs.
  3. $1–2k a month in owner cash after costs.
  4. Enough to cover a meaningful household bill.
  5. Enough to carry the owner through a job gap.
  6. Progressive salary replacement.

## The model: a portfolio of small bets

- Always be scanning for niches. Keep several opportunities in the pipeline at once.
- Enter cheaply, harvest while the window is open, and exit when it closes.
- **Exit means sell when possible** (for example on Acquire.com or Flippa), otherwise shut down cleanly. Build every product so it can be sold: separate accounts, clean books, documented operations, no dependence on the owner's personal identity.
- Replace fading income streams with new ones before they run out.

## Owner profile

- Day job: manufacturing IT at a large automaker. Supports a MES platform across 30+ plants. Works with SQL, IT/OT integrations, back-end troubleshooting, PowerShell, HMI/SCADA and OPC communication.
- Follows AI tools and agent tooling closely, almost nightly.
- Availability: about 2 hours a day, sometimes more, variable because of job and on-call duties.
- Role: funder, decision-maker and escalation point. Agents do the routine work.
- Open to any niche, including ones unrelated to manufacturing. Strongest likely edge: software and systems integration, and practical AI applied to messy real-world systems.

## Constraints

- **Everything must be legal.** That includes taxes, business registration and licensing, sales tax on digital products, privacy law, and platform terms.
- **Platform and payment rules are a practical filter.** A legal business that payment processors or marketplaces won't support is not viable here.
- **Budget:** $50–100 a month to start. It can grow once the portfolio is earning enough to fund it. Every recurring cost needs an owner and a reason.
- **Very low owner customer contact.** Agents handle customer communication. The owner handles escalations only.
- **Employer:** the owner handles employment-policy questions personally. Agents must never use employer code, data, equipment, systems, documents or customer relationships.

## Preferences that follow from the constraints

- Favor niches with **built-in distribution**: app and plugin marketplaces (Shopify, Chrome, WordPress, Zapier/Make, Microsoft, Atlassian), search traffic for tools people already look for, and communities where useful free tools spread. Paid ads and outbound sales don't fit the budget or the contact constraint.
- Favor self-serve products with a low exception rate over anything requiring onboarding calls, custom work or high-stakes support.
- Build an **owned audience** in each niche: collect emails from interested buyers (with consent), so the next product in that niche doesn't start from zero. Marketplaces rent us buyers; a list is ours.
- Use a **merchant of record** (such as Paddle or Lemon Squeezy) for payments, so sales tax, VAT, refunds and chargebacks are handled by the payment provider.
